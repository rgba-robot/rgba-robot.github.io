Abstract

Almost all manipulation tasks involve occluded contact dynamics: it is inherently difficult to visually observe detailed surface contacts as they are created and changed. Consider grasping, assembly, peeling, or pushing - even a planar task like "Push T" hides the contact between object and surface. Such contact, however, generates \textit{vibroacoustic} sound through friction and provides useful contact information. In this paper, we explore how a low-cost acoustic camera can perceive occluded contact dynamics to enhance robot manipulation. We use microphone array beamforming to localize audio over time in a 2D RGB camera image plane. When overlaid on video, we call this an \textit{RGBA} video stream (\textit{A} for Acoustic). We present two approaches to using RGBA video: 1) RGBA-Attention and 2) RGBA-History. Across five contact-rich manipulation tasks, experiments suggest that acoustic video improves task success rate by 20.5% and progress by 17.5% on average compared to Vision-Only and mono-channel audio approach baselines.

Acoustic Camera Setup

A low-cost microphone array captures the sound field around the workspace. Combined with a co-registered RGB camera, we can localize sound sources over time and project them onto the same image plane the policy already sees.

  • Microphone array — off-the-shelf, calibrated to the RGB frame
  • Acoustic beamforming — spatial audio in the image plane
  • RGBA overlay — a drop-in replacement for RGB observations

How Beamforming Works

Two Methods for Learning from RGBA

We present two approaches to leverage acoustic video for imitation learning policies.

1 · RGBA-History

Accumulated acoustic video is used directly as a policy observation, letting the model track task progress over time from the growing evidence of contact events.

2 · RGBA-Attention

RGBA frames are accumulated in the opposite direction and remaining acoustic signals are max-pooled onward. We then supervise the model's visual attention to focus computation on active contact regions.
